Backtest — 103 races, 2022–2026, walk-forward

No model beats "the pole sitter wins." That naive rule scores 0.573 top-1 over 103 races. It is on the table below, and it is the number to look at first.

Intervals are 95% bootstrap, resampling races. At roughly 22 races a season, a single-season accuracy carries a standard error near 10 points — which is why this spans five seasons rather than one.

How it works. Data comes from the Jolpica-F1 API (the successor to Ergast, which shut down in early 2025) and weather from Open-Meteo. Both are free and need no API key. A scheduled job refetches the calendar every run, refreshes the dataset, settles finished races, and predicts the next one.

Why you can trust the track record. Predictions are committed to git before the race takes place, so the commit history is the evidence that nothing was fitted after the fact.

Honest limitation. Backtest weather comes from reanalysis — what actually happened during the race — while a live prediction only has a forecast. Backtest figures involving weather are therefore an upper bound on what is achievable at prediction time.
